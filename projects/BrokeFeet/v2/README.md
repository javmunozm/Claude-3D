# BrokeFeetV1

Rebuild of the BrokeFeet clubfoot teaching model, segmented so the hollow-bone
defects never form — rather than being patched afterwards.

## Why a V1 rather than a fix to BrokeFeet

The original model has open windows into hollow bone interiors, most visibly on
the navicular / talar head. Every post-hoc repair was tried and measured, and
they cannot work. The reason is not effort, it is that both available
discriminators point the wrong way:

| Site | Mean grey | Must be |
|---|---|---|
| Hollow calcaneal interior | **96.5** | filled |
| Ankle mortise (tibiotalar joint) | **105.2** | left open |
| Tarsal rind | **111.2** | left open |

The void that must be filled is **darker** than the joint that must never be
filled — best single-threshold separation is Youden J = 0.002, with 67 %
histogram overlap. Any brightness gate fills the ankle first and welds the
tibia to the talus.

Enclosure is inverted the same way, structurally: a hollow interior is hollow
*because* its cortical rim is broken, so it never reads as enclosed; a joint
space is flanked by two intact cortical plates, so it always does. Enclosure
tests select for joints and reject the defects.

Measured at the operator-located navicular defect (−118.74, −128.10, 66.66),
material missing versus a locally convex surface:

| Ball radius | Missing | % missing |
|---|---|---|
| 6 mm | 746 mm³ | 43.8 % |
| 8 mm | 1596 mm³ | 40.1 % |
| 10 mm | 2973 mm³ | 39.2 % |
| 12 mm | 4868 mm³ | 39.1 % |

The fraction holds at ~40 % out to 12 mm, so the defect is wider than 24 mm.
It is also part of a continuous 23.4 cm³ air space that drains into the ankle
mortise, which is why no gated fill can reach it in isolation.

## The actual root cause

`segment_axial.py:544`

```python
mask = volume >= BONE_THRESHOLD    # BONE_THRESHOLD = 115
```

One global threshold turns grey into bone/air. Trabecular bone in this
patient's tarsus images at 82–103. It is discarded as air. Where the thin
adolescent cortex is *also* broken across slices, the interior opens to the
outside — the defect the operator photographs.

**It is not a slice-ordering problem.** Order was cross-validated between two
independent series to 0.6 mm RMS, with the main/proximal seam agreeing to
0.07 × 0.19 mm. Re-feeding the same images in any order meets the same
threshold and reproduces the same holes.

## Approach

Two stages, replacing one global decision with a local one:

| Stage | Script | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Feed | `macros/feed_slices.py` | De-identifies all three series, orders the stack, interpolates the 1.25 mm uncaptured gap, emits a per-slice manifest, and self-verifies seam continuity and monotonic Z |
| Segment | `macros/segment_v1.py` | Locally adaptive threshold — hysteresis growth seeded in certain cortex and grown inward through contiguous trabecular material, never across a gap |

Hysteresis is the leading candidate because it decides by **connectivity to a
seeded bone** rather than by brightness — which is the one axis where a
trabecular interior and a joint space genuinely differ.

### Radius is per-crater, not global

The BrokeFeet README swept radius on ONE crater and concluded 2.5 mm was the
only value leaving topology untouched. Swept over all six located craters, the
cliff is a property of the individual crater:

| Crater | best r | crater removed | 2.5 mm costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| op1 tarsal | 2.5 mm | 54 % | — |
| op2 near mortise | 3.0 mm | 29 % | −1 tunnel (fine) |
| op3 lateral | 2.5 mm | 80 % | — |
| op4 medial | **1.5 mm** | 62 % | **+2 tunnels** |
| op5 plantar | 2.5 mm | 81 % | — |
| navicular | **3.5 mm** | 13 % | **+4 tunnels** |

A single global radius is the wrong shape for this parameter. Note also that
patches **interact**: applied cumulatively, op1's own removal fell from +54 %
to −16 % because neighbouring patches deepened its pocket. Removal is not
additive and must be re-measured after the full set, never per crater alone.

### The pillar side effect is real, and smaller than recorded

BrokeFeet rejected this patch because it planted a 5th pillar at
(111.8, −126.8). Confirmed — a new pillar does appear at (111.8, −126.2). But
filling the craters also **removes three** pillars that stood in them, so the
net is **4 → 2**, and adding that coordinate to `SKIP_PILLAR_REGIONS_MM` takes
it to **1**. The patch removes more scaffolding than it creates.

**Frame check (the documented trap):** the crater is at mesh X = −114.8 and the
pillar prints at X = +111.8. The sign flip is `MIRROR_X`, so printed pillar
coordinates are in the scan's own pre-mirror frame and go into
`SKIP_PILLAR_REGIONS_MM` directly, matching the existing (135.2, −57.8) entry.

### A seventh discriminator, and it fails like the other six

`macros/crater_map.py` ranks voids by how *recessed* they are — distance
through free space to genuine open air, which should favour a blind pocket over
an open slot. It does not: the ankle mortise (MUST STAY OPEN) is **rank 0**, the
single most recessed void in the model (2944 mm³, max depth 13.8 mm), while the
operator's craters rank 4, 9, 11 and 42. Add it to the list of discriminators
that rank the must-not-fill site above the must-fill one.

`macros/probe_blob_identity.py` shows why no rule can work: **all nine named
sites lie in ONE background component of 5824 cm³.** Must-fill and must-not-fill
are literally the same air space. Separation can only be geometric — a named
box — which is exactly what makes `_patch_named_craters` the right tool and
every global rule the wrong one.

### Verified independently of the agent that produced it

Re-measured on the two exported STLs directly: genus **79 → 72**, volume
**120.64 → 121.29 cm³**, watertight **True**, bodies **1**. The before/after
renders were also read rather than trusted — `renders/cmp_before` vs
`cmp_after` show the dark tarsal fissures gone with the surrounding contours

## Solid model for FreeCAD: `cad/BrokeFeetV1_bone_solid.step`

BrokeFeet's README states that a CT reconstruction cannot become an editable
solid and that no export format fixes it. **The format half of that claim was
never tested** — `export_step.py` existed but `BrokeFeet/cad/` was empty.
Measured:

| | trimesh decimation | **manifold3d** |
|---|---|---|
| Decimation result | 124 broken faces at 25k | watertight, genus preserved |
| OCC sewing | **12 shells** (3 closed, 9 open) | **1 closed shell** |
| STEP entity written | `SHELL_BASED_SURFACE_MODEL` | **`MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP`** |

The blocker was never the file format. `trimesh.simplify_quadric_decimation`
punches holes in the mesh, so OCC receives disjoint fragments and can only emit
a surface model; `trimesh.repair.fill_holes` does not close them (euler −52).
`manifold3d.Manifold.simplify(0.15)` preserves **genus and volume exactly** at
0.15 mm max deviation, and OCC then builds a true solid.

Tolerance is a cliff, not a knob — face count plateaus at ~122k and anything
looser breaks watertightness, so ~300 MB is the floor for a topologically
correct solid at full genus:

| tol | tris | genus | vol cm³ | watertight | max dev |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.15** | 122,416 | **79** | **120.6** | **True** | 0.15 mm |
| 0.18–0.22 | ~122,300 | 47 | 120.6 | False | 0.18 mm |
| 0.3 | 59,556 | 50 | 111.2 | False | 4.72 mm |

**What this does and does not give you.** One freeform solid body with no
feature tree: booleans, sectioning, cutting and measurement work in FreeCAD;
sketch-driven parametric editing does not, and does not exist for scan-derived
geometry in any tool. Expect a slow first open. Regenerate with
`macros/export_solid_step.py <in.stl> <out.step>`.

As shipped, built from `BrokeFeetV1_craters_final.stl`: **297 MB**, 120,208
`ADVANCED_FACE`, and — the check that matters — **1 `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP`,
1 `CLOSED_SHELL`, 0 `OPEN_SHELL`**. Sewing takes ~4 minutes. If a future export
reports `SHELL ONLY`, the input mesh was not watertight; fix the mesh rather
than the exporter.

### Why Taubin, and why gentle

Laplacian smoothing shrinks a closed surface monotonically, which thins cortex
and **widens** joints — the one failure mode that matters here. Taubin's
alternating λ/−μ pass band leaves low-frequency shape alone. Strength matters:
at λ=0.5 μ=0.53 ×10 the mortise went 1.000 → 2.000 (joint opening up) and
volume fell 0.956 %. λ=0.20 μ=0.21 ×5 holds both.

Fine terracing remains on the talar body and other flat regions — this was
deliberately the mildest setting swept, so the pass is a real partial result,
not a finished one.

**Judged insufficient by the operator (2026-08-12), and smoothing is closed as
an approach.** Stronger settings are not the answer: λ=0.5 μ=0.53 ×10 already
widened the mortise 1.000 → 2.000, i.e. it opens joints before it finishes
de-terracing. The remaining terracing is being handled by manual sculpting in
Blender instead — see the handoff section below.

### The gates had to be rebuilt, and three attempts were wrong

The voxel gates in `patch_craters_test.py` (`gap_at`, tunnels, bone count) run
on the iso array and **cannot** be applied after meshing. Building mesh-space
equivalents failed three times, each worth recording:

1. **Single-axis ray cast** — reported the mortise at 1.200 mm where the voxel
   gate measures 3.000. Wrong by 2.5×, and wrong in the *unsafe* direction: it
   would equally have mis-reported a joint being welded shut. It also reported
   a 7 mm "improvement" on a pass whose mean vertex travel was 0.19 mm, which
   is what exposed it.
2. **`trimesh.contains()` on a sub-grid** — exhausts memory against 459k faces
   however the queries are batched or the mesh is cropped.
3. **Re-voxelising and rebuilding the index mapping by hand** — ignored the
   axis permutation `_mesh_to_index` documents (grid axis 2 carries X, axis 1
   carries −Y, axis 0 carries Z), the exact trap that docstring warns about.

What works: re-voxelise onto the pipeline's own grid via `to_index()` and reuse
`patch_craters_test.gap_at` unchanged. But `trimesh.voxelize().fill()` marks
every voxel the surface passes through, adding a ~1-voxel skin — measured
**recall 0.9994, precision 0.9010** against the reference grid. That skin coats
both walls of a joint, so the 3.000 mm mortise reads as 1.000. The bias is
systematic, so it cancels in a comparison: **both meshes go through the
identical round trip and the gate is on the change, never the absolute value.**
Round-trip widths in the table above are therefore *not* comparable to the
3.000 / 2.000 mm voxel-grid numbers elsewhere in this README.

### A global max() is the wrong shape for a travel gate

The first travel gate was `max(travel) < 1.5 mm` and it failed at every setting
swept, down to λ=0.2 ×5 where mean travel is 0.093 mm. The cause is not filter
strength: **20 vertices out of 229,243 (0.009 %)** sit at the bottom of one
narrow crevice near (−102, −157, 84…92), where smoothing pulls opposing walls
together. Valence is normal (5–9), so this is not degenerate topology.

Over 229k vertices a global max is a single-outlier test, not a shape-integrity
test. The gate is now p99/p99.9 against the 0.5 mm terrace height, with genus
and body count carrying the "did a gap weld through" question — which is what
they actually answer.

Two things are done to the mesh, both exactly invertible:

  sculpt pivot and brush falloff misbehave 180 mm from the origin.
- **Scaled ×0.001** with scene units set to millimetres, so Blender's readout
  shows true mm and brush radii are sane. (Blender's unit is the metre; a

bbox back to the original mm coordinates.

    python macros/import_from_blender.py <your_sculpt.stl> --out sculpted_mm.stl

### Voxel remesh was planned, and is a measured negative

Uniform topology is what sculpt brushes want — this mesh runs 0.07–4.65 mm edge
lengths, a 65× range, so the same stroke deforms it unevenly. Voxel remesh was
therefore the plan. It cannot be used: it closes this model's thin interosseous
tunnels at **every** pitch tried.

| voxel | faces | bodies | genus | vs source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| source | 458 770 | 1 | **72** | — |
| 0.40 mm | 707 256 | 7 | 17 | 55 tunnels gone |
| 0.30 mm | 1 263 476 | 1 | 30 | 42 tunnels gone |
| 0.25 mm | 1 822 516 | 11 | 18 | 54 tunnels gone |
| 0.20 mm | 2 840 388 | 1 | 29 | 43 tunnels gone |

Genus never recovers, so this is not a resolution knob — the remesher cannot
represent walls that thin. **Volume moved less than 0.02 % throughout**, so a
volume check would have passed all four; only genus and body count caught it.

Centre + scale alone is exact (genus 72, bbox delta 0.0001 mm), so the original
triangles ship. For uneven brush behaviour use **Dyntopo** (Ctrl+D in the
viewport, preset to 0.4 mm) which refines locally under the brush, or remesh
only a masked region — both leave the rest of the topology intact.

### Dyntopo was off, and the vertex count is why that matters

The first handoff had a bug: `constant_detail_resolution` is a **divisor of the
Blender unit** (soft range 0.001–1000, default 3), and because the prep script
scaled the model to metres, a 0.4 mm detail size computed to **2500** — past the
soft maximum. X-symmetry was also left at Blender's default of ON, which on a
left clubfoot mirrors strokes onto the wrong side. Both are fixed in
`macros/prep_for_blender.py`: no scaling (1 unit = 1 mm, so the divisor is 2.5),
symmetry off, Dyntopo enabled in the file rather than left to Ctrl+D.

The sculpting still worked — brushes displace existing vertices without Dyntopo;
they just cannot add topology. **That is exactly what made it invisible to the
obvious checks.** Vertex count, face count, bounding box, and centroid were all
identical to nine decimals, and on that basis the sculpt was first reported as
not having happened. Comparing the raw triangle arrays is what found it:
30 448 triangles differing, up to 7.13 mm.

**Never conclude "no change" from counts and bounds on a sculpted mesh.** Diff
the triangles.

## Hallux joint pinned (`BrokeFeetV2_pinned.stl`)

`macros/pin_hallux_joint.py`.

The 1st MTP joint — big toe, proximal phalanx base against the 1st metatarsal
head — was held together by a bridge **0.295 mm** across at
(−89.51, −55.06, 35.38). The mesh is one connected body, so every topology gate
passed, but 0.295 mm is under a single 0.4 mm extrusion width: the slicer
renders it as a thread or drops it, the phalanx reads as floating, and it snaps
on handling.

Fixed with an internal **3.0 mm × 10.0 mm cylinder** spanning the joint, sunk
into both bones. Joint cross-section 147.79 → 151.46 mm². Watertight, 1 body,
volume 122.200 → 122.232 cm³. `tools/render_check.py` passes.

This is a **printability reinforcement, not an anatomical correction** — the
joint space stays visible from outside; only the interior is bridged.

### Fitting the axis matters more than the diameter

Three axis choices, measured by how much of the pin ends up inside bone:

| axis | inside |
|---|---|
| closest-approach normal | local surface artefact, unusable |
| metatarsal centroid → phalanx centroid | **58 %** |
| centroids of bone within 9 mm of the joint | **90 %** |

Both bones curve away from the joint, so a full centroid-to-centroid line is
dragged off-axis and the pin exits through the side as a visible strut. Fitting
to material near the joint keeps it buried. The script prints an axial profile
(`I` = inside bone, `.` = open space) and warns if an open span reaches a pin
end; the correct result is one gap at the joint and nothing else:

    IIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIII........IIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIII

Other joints in the model may be similarly thin — this script only treats the
hallux, which is the one the operator identified.

## 2nd MTP joint pinned (`BrokeFeetV2_pinned2.stl`)

`macros/pin_2nd_mtp_joint.py`, run against `BrokeFeetV2_pinned.stl` (sculpt +
hallux pin).

The operator circled a second joint in a render, on a lesser-toe ray adjacent
to the already-pinned hallux. Measured, **all four lesser-toe MTP joints are
comparably fragile** to the hallux's 0.295 mm bridge — this is a pipeline-wide
artefact of the segmentation threshold, not unique to one toe:

| ray | MTP joint gap | site (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (2nd toe) | 0.276 mm | (−103.23, −68.42, 26.39) |
| 2 (3rd toe) | 0.283 mm | (−118.46, −69.83, 14.98) |
| 3 (4th toe) | 0.260 mm | (−120.60, −76.87, 15.00) |
| 4 (5th toe) | 0.297 mm | (−137.52, −71.43, 9.79) |

The 2nd ray (ray 1) matches the operator's circled site and is the only one
pinned so far. **The other three lesser-toe joints remain unpinned** and are
equally fragile by this measurement — treat them the same way if they turn
out to matter for handling/printing.

This joint's geometry differs from the hallux's: it is condylar (the phalanx
base sits offset from the metatarsal head's centroid line, not directly
in-line), so the hallux script's direct local-centroid axis method only put
29–34% of the pin inside bone here, with the open span reaching a pin end at
every radius tried. The fix that worked: fit the axis direction the same way,
then **re-centre the pin on the actual measured open-air span** (a grid search
perpendicular to the axis, then along it) rather than trusting the raw
closest-approach point or centroid midpoint — both are surface artefacts
offset from the true bone bulk at this joint.

Diameter and length interact here more than at the hallux. A 6 mm pin needed
1.5 mm diameter to keep its surface (not just its axis) inside bone at every
angle — 2.0 mm and 2.5 mm at that length left edge rays poking out. But 1.5 mm
mostly-buried risks vanishing under the slicer's line width. Lengthening to
8 mm let a 2.5 mm diameter pin's ends sit in thicker bone further from the
joint waist: **2.5 mm × 8 mm passes the 24-angle surface check clean, with no
visible strut in render**, and is the shipped default.

### The first attempt reopened the craters, and every gate passed

The first version used a 2.5 mm core / 4.0 mm falloff with no directional
constraint. The operator rejected it on sight — it made *"other craters
reappear"* — and the geometry confirms it exactly:

| | first attempt |
|---|---|
| Verts pulled inward > 0.5 mm | **304** |
| > 1.0 mm | 84 |
| Worst | **−3.61 mm** |
| Net volume | **−0.093 cm³** (material the operator added, taken back out) |

Two clusters sat directly on hand-filled craters: 98 verts sinking 3.39 mm at
3.0 mm from op1, and 180 verts sinking 3.61 mm near the ankle.

The cause is intrinsic to smoothing, not to the radius: **a Taubin pass pulls a
convex fill down toward its concave surroundings**, which is precisely a crater
returning. Shrinking the band cannot fix it.

The fix is a directional clamp — the inward-normal component of the correction
is dropped, so a seam blend can only ever shave a proud rim, never restore a
cavity. Worst inward travel is now −0.000009 mm (float noise), and volume rises
rather than falls.

**All eight original gates passed while this was happening.** Volume is far too
coarse to see it: refilling a crater costs a few mm³ against 122 000, and the
first attempt's −0.076 % sailed through a ±0.5 % limit. Only signed normal
travel catches it, and that gate now exists.

Why not another global pass: `smooth_terracing.py` was already judged
insufficient and stronger settings open the mortise (λ=0.5 μ=0.53 ×10 took it
1.000 → 2.000). A global filter also has no reason to concentrate effort where
the seams are.
